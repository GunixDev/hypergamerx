## Pong (1972)

The first video game that became popular worldwide. Pong not only opened the door to the video game industry but also established the sports game genre. Its simplicity and addictiveness turned it into a cultural phenomenon, capturing the attention of millions of players worldwide. With its innovative concept of virtually playing tennis, Pong laid the groundwork for the development of future video games and marked the beginning of a new era in interactive entertainment. Although the graphics were simple and the controls limited, the experience of playing Pong was revolutionary in its time and set the foundation for the evolution of the video game industry.

## Super Mario Bros (1985)

This game not only saved the video game industry after the crash of 1983 but also set the standard for all platform games that would come afterward. Super Mario Bros became a cultural phenomenon and an industry icon, propelling Nintendo to success and revitalizing the video game market. With its innovative gameplay, masterfully designed levels, and charismatic characters like Mario and Luigi, the game captivated players of all ages. Furthermore, Super Mario Bros introduced elements such as power-ups, secret levels, and the ability to explore different worlds, laying the foundation for the development of future platform games. Its impact on the industry was so significant that its legacy endures to this day, with numerous sequels and spin-offs that continue to enchant players worldwide.

<Imageresp image="7videogames-3_ceuzgz.jpg"/>

## The Legend of Zelda (1986)

The first game of its kind, The Legend of Zelda introduced the concept of an open and non-linear world, where players could explore at their own pace. With its vast and mysterious realm of Hyrule, full of secrets and challenges, the game broke away from the traditional structure of video games at the time. Instead of following a linear narrative, players had the freedom to immerse themselves in a world full of possibilities. They could traverse extensive landscapes, discover hidden dungeons, and face formidable enemies. Additionally, The Legend of Zelda introduced innovative elements such as the saving system, item acquisition, and puzzle-solving, which would become pillars of the series. Its influence can be seen in numerous later games that adopted the open-world approach, allowing players to engage in immersive and personalized gaming experiences.

## Doom (1993)

Doom not only popularized the first-person shooter genre but also introduced the concept of multiplayer networking, forever changing the way we play video games. This iconic 1993 game revolutionized the industry by offering an intense and frenetic gaming experience, where players immersed themselves in a world full of demons and unrestrained action. However, its impact was not limited solely to its innovative gameplay. Doom pioneered the development of online game modes, allowing players to face each other in thrilling multiplayer battles. This groundbreaking feature opened the doors to a new way of playing, connecting players from around the world and laying the groundwork for the popularity of online games that we see today. Additionally, Doom was also one of the first games to allow the creation and customization of levels through map editors, leading to an active community of players who shared their creations and expanded the gaming experience. In summary, Doom not only left an indelible mark on the history of video games but also changed the way we interact with and enjoy them.

## Final Fantasy VII (1997)

This game not only revolutionized RPGs with its deep narrative and memorable characters but also showcased the potential of 3D graphics in video games. Released in 1997, Final Fantasy VII set a milestone in the gaming industry by combining an epic and emotional story with impressive 3D visuals. The game featured charismatic characters such as Cloud Strife and Sephiroth, whose stories and conflicts became benchmarks for the genre. Moreover, Final Fantasy VII introduced innovations in the combat system, allowing players to customize their characters and use special abilities in strategic battles. However, its impact extended beyond gameplay and narrative. The detailed graphics and 3D environments of Final Fantasy VII demonstrated the potential of technology at that time, paving the way for future advances in the visual design of video games. Its influence can be seen in numerous RPGs and adventure games that followed its lead, setting new standards in terms of narrative, characters, and visual presentation. In summary, Final Fantasy VII left an indelible mark on the gaming industry, solidifying its position as one of the most influential titles of all time.

## The Sims (2000)

The Sims changed the way we think about video games, proving that they don't need to be about winning or losing but can simply be a simulation of life. Released in the year 2000, this groundbreaking simulation game allowed players to create and control their own virtual characters, known as Sims, and guide them through their daily lives. Unlike other games that focused on clear objectives and competitive challenges, The Sims offered a more relaxed experience centered around exploration and creativity. Players could design houses, build relationships, seek employment, and fulfill the basic needs of their Sims, all while observing how their virtual lives unfolded. This unique and non-linear approach broke away from the conventions of traditional video games and opened the doors to a new way of playing. The Sims became a cultural phenomenon, attracting players of all ages and genders. Its success led to numerous expansions and sequels, further expanding the possibilities of gameplay and solidifying its status as one of the most influential games of all time. In summary, The Sims demonstrated that video games can be a form of artistic expression and a tool for exploring life and creativity, leaving a lasting mark on the interactive entertainment industry.

## Minecraft (2011)

Minecraft revolutionized the video game industry with its focus on creativity and construction, allowing players to create their own worlds and experiences. Since its launch in 2011, this open-world game has captivated millions of players worldwide with its limitless freedom and unique pixelated aesthetic. In Minecraft, players can explore a vast and generative world, collect resources, and use them to build various structures, from simple cabins to entire cities. The ability to create and customize every aspect of the environment, combined with the option to play in single-player or multiplayer mode, has turned Minecraft into an unparalleled platform for creative expression. Moreover, the game has fostered an active community of players who share their creations, mods, and adventures, further expanding the possibilities of the game. Its success has transcended the boundaries of the video game industry, becoming a cultural and educational phenomenon used in schools and museums worldwide. In summary, Minecraft has left an indelible mark on the industry by showcasing the power of creativity and construction in video games, inspiring players of all ages to imagine and create their own virtual worlds.
